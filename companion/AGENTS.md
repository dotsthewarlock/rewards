## Engineering standard
Prioritize lean, cohesive modular, scalable, resilient, maintainable, token-efficient AI-first development. AI-first means development and handoffs, not runtime AI. Optimize the total lifecycle cost: code, dependencies, context, tests, diagnosis, maintenance and recovery. Use direct native modules and explicit contracts; one owner for each business rule and state transition. Extract only for a real independent reason to change, trust/lifecycle boundary or measurable maintenance benefit. Prefer existing tooling, bounded tests, deterministic fixtures, straightforward data flow and minimal sufficient changes. No arbitrary file-count or code-sharing target, parallel production engines, speculative abstractions, repeated foundation rebuilds or extra automation infrastructure.
- Balance prevention with recovery according to impact, likelihood, detectability, reversibility and user recovery friction. For low-impact reversible failures, prefer simple detection, clear safe stop, bounded retry where unambiguously safe, idempotent restart, backup/rollback and targeted repair over complex prevention. For irreversible external effects, duplicate claims, credential/privacy leaks, data loss or uncertain submission, prevention and fail-closed invariants are mandatory; recovery is complementary, never an excuse to weaken them. Never automatically retry an ambiguous claim. Durable minimal evidence and explicit reconciliation beat fragile elaborate orchestration. Test actual failure/recovery paths. Record material trade-offs and residual risk.
- Design for expected growth (new campaigns, long queues, both platforms), not hypothetical expansion. Reassess when evidence warrants change; avoid adding layers solely for future possibility. Limit broad rereads; follow symbols, relevant diffs and tests, use source contracts and exact commit links instead of copying chat history.
